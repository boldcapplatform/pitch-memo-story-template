# Example engagement (abridged)

Shows the expected shape of pitch-story output. Fictional company throughout. Note what the skill does at each step: asks before writing, sorts ruthlessly, names gaps, drafts only from excavated material.

## Founder's opening message

"We're Ledgerline — AI-powered bookkeeping for construction firms. Revolutionary accuracy, massive market, raising a $2M seed. Can you help with our pitch?"

## What the skill does first (not drafting)

Asks, a few at a time: What do you believe about bookkeeping for construction that accountants would argue with? Walk me through one contractor's Tuesday before Ledgerline. What did you refuse to build? Which architecture did you reject, and what does your eval suite measure? Who found you without being sold — what were their exact words?

## Sorted piles (shown to the founder)

**Fingerprints**
- Turned down a 400-seat GC because they wanted project-management features — cost ~$70k ARR. ("The Choice")
- Rejected retrieval-over-the-ledger architecture after 6 weeks; rebuilt on a typed transaction graph. Built a 1,100-case reconciliation eval; 96.4% vs 71% for the GPT-wrapper baseline. ("What Can't Be Cloned", appendix)
- Three of first five customers came from one subcontractor forum thread; quote: "first tool that knows what a lien waiver is." ("The Tribe")

**Claims → challenged**
- "Revolutionary accuracy" → replaced by the eval number.
- "Massive market" → cut; replaced by the forum-thread pull as evidence a tribe exists.

**Gaps (pre-raise homework)**
- No hire passed on yet — the bar hasn't been tested. Do one hard pass before pitching team quality.
- Villain scene is secondhand. Founder to sit in a contractor's office on a month-end close and get the scene firsthand.

## The Retell

1. Construction firms lose real money because general-purpose bookkeeping doesn't understand construction's primitives — draws, liens, retainage.
2. Ledgerline built a transaction graph that speaks those primitives natively, and an eval suite that proves it reconciles what generic AI tools get wrong a third of the time.
3. Subcontractors are already finding it through word of mouth, and the round funds keeping their standard — no project-management creep — while the tribe compounds.

## Chapter 5 — The Choice (as drafted into template.md)

**Point**: We refused to become project-management software, at a price.
**Proof**: Walked away from a 400-seat GC and ~$70k ARR rather than build their feature list.
**Handoff**: What we built instead is the reason the product feels different in the first thirty seconds.
