# The Story Deck — [Company]

This is the default output structure: use it whenever the founder has no structure in mind. It is one cut of the story graph — ten chapters, numbered 01–10, in the order a partner's mind actually moves: world → timing → problem → bets → product → pull → moat → growth → team → ask. The chosen arc may reorder it (an origin arc leads with 09; a craft arc leads with 04) — reorder chapters, never break their internal anatomy.

**Every chapter carries the same anatomy**, whatever the output style (deck copy, written memo, quiet-narrative HTML, or dossier):

- **Hook** — one line that frames what the chapter must prove. Opens the section.
- **Point** — the single claim of the chapter, in the company's words.
- **Proof** — the fingerprint, eval, or voice that carries the point. One proof proves; two decorate; three stall.
- **Takeaway** — one or two liftable sentences a skimming partner can carry whole. Closes the section.
- **Handoff** — the line that makes the next chapter necessary.

The meta-line under each chapter title shows what the skill routes into it: the old slide it replaces, the story mode it runs, the unlock it carries, the taste-ledger components (#) that feed it, and the presentation pattern (`references/presentation.md`) it follows. If the founder's material doesn't clear that pattern's Context Bar, the Proof line carries a Context Request, not a draft.

Rules for the whole document: fill only from excavated material. No superlatives, no analogy as load-bearing argument, no TAM theater. Read the ten chapter titles alone — they must tell the whole story. The skim path (hooks + takeaways only) must retell the pitch.

## What the Reader Must Believe (the spine)

The two or three load-bearing beliefs an investor must accept for this to be a fundable round — the memo's coherence spine, and the executive summary's core. Name each, and attach one line of proof beneath it: a fact, a customer's behavior, an eval — never an assertion. Every chapter below defends exactly one belief; a chapter that defends none gets cut. Read these beliefs, then the chapter titles in order: the titles should be these beliefs, paid off one at a time.

- **Belief 1** — _____ **· Proof:** _____
- **Belief 2** — _____ **· Proof:** _____
- **Belief 3** — _____ **· Proof:** _____

*A belief with no proof yet is not a weakness to hide; write it as a marked gap. An honestly flagged gap survives diligence — a smoothed assertion does not.*
